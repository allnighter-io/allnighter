The real cards are computed, not typed. Codex 82% is the mean peak of its two well-observed completed windows (64% and 100%, from 665 and 1,858 readings). Grok 54% is the mean of two completed weeks (41% and 66%). Everything else genuinely has no completed window yet.

Codex's week re-bases rather than repeating. Its resets_at drifts every cycle — the 7-day clock restarts on first use after the previous window closes, so its weeks never align to calendar weeks. Grok's are fixed Thu→Thu. This is the strongest argument yet for cards over a chart: an average over "windows" does not care when they happened, and a time axis would have had to reconcile floating boundaries against fixed ones.

Coverage filtering is load-bearing. Several Codex windows were seen only once, at 0%, immediately after a reset. Counting those as "used nothing" would have pulled the average from 82% to 49% and inverted the conclusion from upgrade to downgrade. A window is only counted when it was observed close to its end.

Hitting a limit is a win when the bench is deep. With six seats, maxing one out and moving to the next is full extraction — that is the target state, not a warning. The only costly version is hitting a limit with nowhere to go. So the card splits them: Codex's 9× · always had a fallback is a victory lap, while Claude's 7× · blocked 4× is the upgrade signal. "Blocked → 0" is precisely what Allnighter is for, which makes this the product's own scorecard.

That split must be captured at the moment of the hit. The ledger has to snapshot every other seat's headroom when a limit event fires. Reconstructing it later from two independent histories with different sampling times is not possible — which is why the real Codex card above says fallback unknown rather than guessing. Cheap to record now, gone forever if CAP-S01 skips it.

The efficiency multiple does the arguing. 91% is 1.1× the cost of full use; 24% is 4.2×; 6% is 17×. It is a fact, not advice — and it is most eloquent at the bottom, where the cancel case makes itself without anyone recommending anything.

Green is back on this tab, deliberately. The capacity strip is an alerting surface where silence means success, so nothing there is green. This is a scorecard, where the achievement should be visible. Two different colour contracts on purpose — do not "fix" the inconsistency later.
